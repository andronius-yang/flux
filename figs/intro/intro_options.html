What the traces actually say

These findings decide which designs are honest and which would show something the data does not contain.

Expert-level skew is large and topic-dependent. On LiveCodeBench the hottest K2 expert is routed 9.5× its uniform share; on MMLU professional law it is 33×. The eight hottest K2 experts absorb 14% of all routed tokens (21% on Qwen), and half of all tokens land on under a fifth of the experts.

Contiguous ownership averages the skew down, and the averaging depends on experts per rank. K2 places 24 experts on each of 16 ranks, so its hottest rank sits at 1.57× uniform on LiveCodeBench even though single experts reach 9.5×. Qwen places only 8 experts per rank, so a flatter expert curve (4.3× peak) still yields a hotter rank: 2.05×. Node-level aggregates flatten further (1.11× K2, 1.35× Qwen). The imbalance lives at the per-GPU NIC, so the second panel must be per rank, not per node.

Under equal per-rank batches the traffic matrix is column-striped. Every source rank samples the same routing distribution, so every row of the 16×16 matrix has the same profile; row sums are exactly tokens × top-k. Egress spans only 0.96–1.05× (the intra-node exclusion is the only variation), while ingress equals the expert load of the destination rank. A dispatch heatmap therefore carries sixteen numbers, however many cells it has. The combine phase mirrors it: the hot rank's egress, not ingress, is the skewed side.

It is not a layer-5 artifact. Across every MoE layer the hottest rank runs 1.27–2.49× on K2 (median 1.66×) and 1.40–2.78× on Qwen (median 1.90×), LiveCodeBench in both cases.

Hotness is stable in time but moves with topic. Two consecutive 32-token decode windows of one topic share 55–85% of their hot-expert set (L1 distance 0.14–0.30). Two topics share 2–37% (K2 L1 0.42–0.97); the hottest K2 NIC is rank 4 on LiveCodeBench, rank 3 on professional law, rank 14 on college math. The same holds on Qwen with four topics.

Should the figure carry two datasets?

Yes, but "two datasets" should mean two topics of one model, not two models. The two models tell the same within-topic story with different averaging (24 versus 8 experts per rank), which is a detail for the evaluation, not the opening claim. Two topics add a claim the single-topic view cannot make: the hot set is specific to the workload, so a placement tuned to yesterday's traffic is wrong for today's. The measured numbers support that cleanly, with a caveat.

  • Cross-topic drift is large. Hot-expert set overlap between K2 topics is 2–37%; on Qwen the four topics overlap 7–33%. The hottest NIC changes rank in every pairing.
  • Within-topic drift is small. Consecutive decode windows overlap 55–85% on the hot set; rank loads move by an L1 of 0.03–0.15. This is why the oracle-basis placement works at all, and why the figure must not overstate temporal drift.
  • The caveat is scope. The default sweeps are single-topic. If the intro promises topic drift, the evaluation section must point at the hetero-oracle and cycling results explicitly, otherwise the promise dangles.

Option C is the two-dataset version; Option A is the single-dataset version. Option D is the two-dataset version of the heatmap, at the cost of a constructed scenario.

Recommendation and next steps

Lead with Option C if the paper keeps its topic-drift results in the evaluation; otherwise Option A. In both, the second panel is per-rank bars with node brackets, with the egress outline kept: it turns the "map onto a topology" step into a visible asymmetry rather than a decorative grid. If the postdoc wants the grid regardless, Option B's K2 panel is the honest single-model form, with the identical rows stated in the caption as a finding.

  1. Pick the design and the models. One model (K2, the larger one) fits a column-width figure; both models need a full-width or two-row figure. The main-perf figure already uses Qwen left, K2 right, so a two-model intro should follow that order.
  2. Pick the topic pair for Option C. LiveCodeBench versus professional law is the extreme pair on K2 (7% overlap); LiveCodeBench versus college math is the mild one (26%). The extreme pair makes the point; the mild pair is more defensible as "typical".
  3. Freeze the numbers. Regenerate the [64, 96) window caches for the second model's remaining layers only if Option E goes in; otherwise the layer strip stays full-pool and says so.
  4. Write the SPEC and generator. Same contract as figs/main_perf: SPEC.md with knobs, make_figure.py in matplotlib at final physical size, fonts embedded, Helvetica/Arial, the muted palette family already ruled for the paper (K2 and Qwen keep their evaluation hues if the figure shows both).
  5. Decide the caption's scope sentence. Whether the figure promises topic drift determines whether the evaluation section must foreground the hetero-oracle and cycling results.
