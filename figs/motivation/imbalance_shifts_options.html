What the three panels claim, and what the traces say

A2AV + GEMM l01_nvshmem

NVSHMEM blocking-put ring dispatch, then un-overlapped grouped GEMM.

  • expert GEMM 1.06–2.18 ms, max/mean 1.50×
  • dispatch span 12.61–13.14 ms, max/mean 1.01×
  • inter-node wire occupancy 6.45–12.03 ms, max/mean 1.32×; barrier wait before GEMM 0.02–6.12 ms, max/mean 1.80×

EPLB eplb_l01_nvplace

static pool-oracle placement (replicated hot experts), direct a2a dispatch, per-slot GEMM.

  • expert GEMM 1.97–2.31 ms, max/mean 1.07×
  • dispatch span 13.05–13.34 ms, max/mean 1.01×
  • a2a kernel + completion barrier 10.19–10.93 ms, max/mean 1.02× (inseparable; per-rank bytes carry the imbalance)
  • placement span 61.05–152.10 ms, max/mean 1.27×; inter-node placement send 0.71–1.37 GB per rank

COMET l01_allgather_dense

dense all-gather: inter-node fetch gate, intra-node P2P, tile-gated fused GEMM.

  • expert GEMM 1.41–3.48 ms, max/mean 1.43×
  • dispatch span 3.23–4.03 ms, max/mean 1.11×
  • inter-node wire occupancy 2.37–3.43 ms, max/mean 1.28×; barrier wait before GEMM 0.46–1.50 ms, max/mean 1.54×
  • barrier wait after GEMM (absorbs the compute spread) 0.00–2.48 ms, max/mean 1.61×
Reading rule. Sender-side dispatch bytes are equal on every rank by construction (each rank routes the same token budget), so dispatch imbalance shows up as receive-side incast and barrier wait, not as unequal send volume. COMET's inter-node fetch is byte-identical on every rank; what the trace shows is that it is fully exposed before the GEMM, while the GEMM itself spreads with the routed rows. EPLB flattens the GEMM and pays for it with a one-shot placement whose per-rank cost tracks how many replicas each home rank pushes.

Option A

Phase strips — one row per rank, phases end to end

The most literal reading of the three-panel brief: each rank is a strip of colored phases; the straggler is the longest strip and the imbalance is the ragged right edge. EPLB gets a second, own-scale axis for the one-shot placement so a 150 ms placement does not flatten a 17 ms step.

Why it works

  • Maps 1:1 onto the narrative: placement → dispatch → compute, and the reader sees which phase grows on each baseline.
  • Per-rank annotations (rows, bytes) sit on the row they explain.
  • Renders at quarter-page height per panel in the paper.

Risks

  • Overlap is invisible: COMET's NVLink copies run under its inter-node fetch, and a single strip has to pick one class per instant (the strip draws the launch order; hover shows the truth).
  • The own-scale placement axis needs a clear break glyph or reviewers will read it as per-step cost.
Option B

Three lanes per rank — NIC, NVLink, SM

Same ranks, same window, but each rank is split into the three resources the story is about. Overlap becomes visible as vertical coincidence: COMET's NVLink lane fills while its NIC lane is busy and its SM lane is empty; the ring's SM lane waits for the last NIC put on every rank; EPLB's SM lane is flat across ranks while its placement lanes are not.

Why it works

  • Shows where the exposed time is: an empty SM lane under a busy NIC lane is the whole COMET argument in one glance.
  • Honest about overlap; nothing is hidden by class priority.
  • Sets up the paper's later figures, which use the same lane vocabulary.

Risks

  • Three lanes × five ranks × three panels is dense at column width; keep four ranks per panel in print.
  • EPLB's direct-a2a kernel mixes NVLink and NIC traffic inside one launch, so its NVLink lane is empty by construction — caption it.
Option C

The imbalance ledger — every rank, three meters

Drops the timeline and keeps only the thesis: for each baseline, where does the per-rank spread live? All 16 ranks are dots on a shared millisecond scale per meter; the ringed dots are the ranks drawn above, so the two views cross-reference. The max/mean label is the number the text can quote.

Why it works

  • Uses all 16 ranks, so no reviewer can ask whether the chosen ranks were cherry-picked.
  • The horizontal shift of the wide cluster from meter to meter across the three rows is the title of the section.
  • Compact: fits in a single column with room for the caption.

Risks

  • Loses the pipeline order; a reader who has not seen a timeline may not know that the dispatch meter precedes the GEMM meter.
  • Placement dots dwarf the others; the scale is per meter, which must be stated.

Rank selection and every labelled number

Selection rule, applied identically per baseline: the two ranks at the extremes of the expert-GEMM duration (most and fewest routed rows), the two at the extremes of the inter-node wire (or, for EPLB, of placement and of dispatch), plus the median-GEMM rank as the typical case. Method notes

  • Capture: one sweep capsule, three arms, modes: [nsys] with FLUX_SWEEP_ISOLATED_ITERS=1; 3 warmup + 3 timed windows; the middle timed window is drawn. Instrumented mode: none of these spans are latency claims (SCHEMA rule 3).
  • Attribution: kernels and copies are joined to the host iterN NVTX range through their launch correlation id. The host range closes at enqueue (a few ms) while the device runs 15–35 ms, so timestamp filtering would drop most of the step.
  • Classes: inter-node wire = NVSHMEM proxy RMA kernels (one per remote put / fetch) and EPLB's direct-a2a kernel; NVLink = CUDA P2P copies; GEMM = the grouped-GEMM launches; wait = barrier and signal-wait kernels; everything else (index builds, packing, routing all-gather) is grey.
  • Bytes: routed rows and per-rank inter-node bytes come from the capsule's traffic matrix (column sums / off-node row sums at 14,336 B per routed token); COMET's inter-node fetch is the fixed remote-shard volume; EPLB's dispatch bytes are the recorded post-placement wire matrix.
  • EPLB placement wire: blocking NVSHMEM puts from each expert's original home (this capsule is the first with the port); the NCCL twin places identical bytes but shows one opaque SendRecv kernel.
